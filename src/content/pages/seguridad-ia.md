## Build the future. Protect what matters.

AI can expand what people learn, create and achieve. Making that progress worthwhile also means asking who could be harmed, how a system might fail and what would help us keep control. At Saturdays.AI, learning to build goes hand in hand with learning to question.

## Two perspectives. One responsibility.

AI Safety asks how we can reduce harmful behaviour and unintended consequences, evaluate capabilities and limitations, and maintain meaningful human oversight.

AI Security also considers deliberate attacks: attempts to manipulate a system, expose sensitive information or misuse its tools and permissions. Protecting an AI application involves its data, software and operating environment as well as the model.

The two perspectives overlap. A useful starting question is: what could go wrong in this particular use, who would be affected, and what evidence would show that a safeguard works?

## Bring these questions to your next project

- Define what the system should do and the actions it should never take on its own.
- Test realistic failures and misuse in an authorised environment, using data you are allowed to use.
- Limit access to data and tools, and decide where a person must review or approve an action.
- Document the limits, monitor what happens and be ready to pause or change the system.

These are starting points for learning. A successful prototype or a course certificate does not establish that a system is safe for every context.

## Keep exploring

The NIST framework offers a structured way to consider risks throughout an AI system's lifecycle. OWASP's guidance introduces practical security risks and mitigations for generative AI applications. These are independent resources for further learning.

- [NIST AI Risk Management Framework](https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework)
- [OWASP guidance for generative AI applications](https://genai.owasp.org/llm-top-10/)
